1. Introduction

Water pollution is one of the most crucial issues in today’s aquatic ecosystem. One way to eliminate water pollution in our community is through filtration methods. Mechanical, UV light, and more are some of the various filtration methods that are used in communities all across the world. Many research papers have shown that the usual solution of boiling your water is simply not enough for most health requirements [1]. Due to the extensive research done by many established scientists, people have switched to water filters. "Water filtration is the process of removing or reducing impurities and contaminants from water, making it safer and cleaner for consumption, cooking, and other uses" [1].

There are numerous environmental contaminants that significantly contribute to human disease, affecting climate change and public and individual health, resulting in increased mortality and morbidity [2]. Although pollutants are not new to the environment, their exposure still poses the greatest threat to humanity and is a significant cause of environmental illness and mortality. Urbanization, industrialization, mining, and exploration are some of the human activities that have had the most impacts on worldwide environmental pollution [3]. This research not only applies to just a specific area. Concentrations of at least one chemical contaminant were greater than an established MCL in 13 percent of the 1,389 wells [4].

Although previous studies have examined the effectiveness of water filtration methods, comparing multiple water filters at a local creek remains understudied, which is effective because it will help those in societies contribute to the purification methods of local water sources. Our project will help people who may not have as much access to clean water verify if their water is clean and safe to drink and use. The present study aims to determine which of five different water filters removes the greatest amount of pollutants from water collected from a local creek. We hypothesized that the activated carbon filter would remove the highest amount of pollutants. This paper discusses the experimental procedure and compares the filtration methods to show the data analysis on which approach is most effective.

Key Terms & Operational Definitions

Internal Cancers Long-term exposure to heavy metals like arsenic and industrial chemicals (PFAS, pesticides) causing genetic mutations.
Pollutants Substances or energy introduced into an environment causing adverse effects on human health or natural ecosystems.
Turbidity A quantitative measure of the cloudiness or haziness of a fluid caused by suspended particulate matter.
Granular Activated Carbon (GAC) A porous carbon filter media designed to adsorb organic chemicals and contaminants from liquid media.
Reverse Osmosis (RO) Systems Forces water under pressure through a semi-permeable membrane to isolate and remove chemical ions.
Ion Exchange Resins Polymeric beads acting as small magnets to exchange non-harmful ions for targeted pollutant ions.

2. Materials & Methods

2.1 Study Design

We executed 5 trials per filter, resulting in 25 trials in total (n = 5 per group). Each trial was conducted under identical environmental conditions using equal volumes of creek water collected simultaneously from the same sampling location.

2.3 Procedures & Statistical Analysis

Water samples were harvested from the designated creek location. Baseline values for turbidity and chemical contamination were recorded on unfiltered water. Each water sample was passed through its designated filter treatment. Post-treatment measurements were recorded in Google Sheets. Inferential statistical evaluation was conducted using a One-Way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) via Statistics Kingdom.
